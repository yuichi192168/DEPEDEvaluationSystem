## What is CAR?

### Definition

**CAR = Comparative Assessment Result**

A professional document showing:
- ✅ All applicants for a position
- ✅ Their evaluation scores
- ✅ Their ranking within the position
- ✅ Complete scoring breakdown
- ✅ Professional format for decision-making

### Purpose

The CAR is used to:
1. **Compare applicants** - See how they rank against each other
2. **Make decisions** - HR uses it to decide who to hire
3. **Document process** - Keeps record of fair evaluation
4. **Justify selections** - Shows objective scoring criteria
5. **Meet compliance** - Follows DepEd HRMPSB standards

### Official Status

- ✅ Recognized by DepEd
- ✅ Part of HRMPSB guidelines
- ✅ Used in official hiring decisions
- ✅ Requires professional formatting
- ✅ Must have authorized signatures

## Header Section

### Header Information Displayed

**Example:**

```
COMPARATIVE ASSESSMENT RESULT - Annex I

Position: Information and Communications Technology
Office/Bureau/Service/Unit: Information and Communications Technology Unit
Plantilla Item Number: n/a
Date of Final Deliberation: January 22, 2026
```

### Each Header Field

| Field | Purpose | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Position | The job being filled | Information and Communications Technology |
| Office/Bureau/Service/Unit | Department/Unit recruiting | ICT Unit |
| Plantilla Item Number | Item number in budget | n/a or 12345 |
| Date of Final Deliberation | When assessment was completed | January 22, 2026 |

### Why This Information?

- **Position:** Identifies which job this CAR covers
- **Office/Bureau:** Shows organizational unit
- **Plantilla Item:** Official budget line item
- **Date:** Records when assessment was done

### Header Formatting

```
Large Title:        "COMPARATIVE ASSESSMENT RESULT - Annex I"
Position Label:     "Position:" followed by name
Office Label:       "Office/Bureau/Service/Unit:" followed by name
Plantilla Label:    "Plantilla Item Number:" followed by number
Date Label:         "Date of Final Deliberation:" followed by date
```

## Data Columns

### All 13 Columns Explained

**Column Layout (Left to Right):**

```
1. NAME
2. CODE
3. Education
4. Training
5. Experience
6. Performance
7. Outstanding Accomplishments
8. Application of Education
9. Application of L&D
10. Potential
11. Total
12. Rank
13. Remarks
```

### Column 1: NAME

**What It Shows:**
- Applicant's full name
- Should exactly match application form

**Example:**
```
Maria Santos
Juan Dela Cruz
Ana Reyes
Carlos Mendoza
```

**Formatting:**
- Regular text
- Full name (first and last)
- Proper capitalization

### Column 2: CODE

**What It Shows:**
- Unique application code
- Identifies applicant
- Used for record-keeping

**Example:**
```
SAMPLE-001
SAMPLE-002
APP-2026-0123
DEPED-HS-456
```

**Important:**
- Must be unique per applicant
- Cannot have duplicates
- Used as reference identifier

### Column 3: Education (0-10)

**What It Shows:**
- Educational qualification score
- Based on degree/certification level
- Relevance to position

**Scoring:**
```
10: Master's/Doctorate in field
9:  Master's/Doctorate related field
8:  Bachelor's in field
7:  Bachelor's related field
6:  Diploma in field
5:  Diploma related field
4:  Vocational training
3:  High school + training
2:  High school only
1:  Some high school
0:  No qualification
```

**Example in CAR:**
```
Maria Santos: 10.0
Juan Dela Cruz: 9.0
Ana Reyes: 8.0
Carlos Mendoza: 7.0
```

### Column 4: Training (0-5)

**What It Shows:**
- Professional training and development score
- Hours, certifications, or programs completed
- Relevance to position

**Scoring:**
```
5: 100+ hours relevant training + certifications
4: 50-99 hours relevant training
3: 25-49 hours relevant training or 1 certification
2: 10-24 hours training or minor certification
1: <10 hours training
0: No training
```

**Example:**
```
Maria Santos: 5.0 (extensive training)
Juan Dela Cruz: 4.0 (moderate training)
Ana Reyes: 3.0 (basic training)
Carlos Mendoza: 2.0 (minimal training)
```

### Column 5: Experience (0-20)

**What It Shows:**
- Years of relevant work experience
- Highest scoring column (double-weighted at 20 points)
- Direct experience in field

**Scoring:**
```
20: 15+ years relevant experience
18: 12-14 years relevant experience
16: 10-11 years relevant experience
14: 8-9 years relevant experience
12: 6-7 years relevant experience
10: 4-5 years relevant experience
8:  2-3 years relevant experience
6:  1 year relevant experience
4:  6-12 months relevant experience
2:  3-6 months relevant experience
0:  No experience
```

**Example:**
```
Maria Santos: 15.0 (15 years)
Juan Dela Cruz: 14.0 (12-14 years)
Ana Reyes: 12.0 (6-7 years)
Carlos Mendoza: 10.0 (4-5 years)
```

### Column 6: Performance (0-25)

**What It Shows:**
- Current job performance rating
- Highest scoring column (25% weight)
- Most important evaluation criterion

**Scoring:**
```
25: Excellent - Exceeds expectations, outstanding
22-24: Very Good - Consistently performs well
19-21: Good - Meets expectations, reliable
16-18: Satisfactory - Meets basic requirements
13-15: Needs Improvement - Below expectations
10-12: Poor - Significant performance issues
0-9:   Unsatisfactory - Failed to perform
```

**Example:**
```
Maria Santos: 9.0 (Very Good)
Juan Dela Cruz: 8.0 (Good)
Ana Reyes: 7.0 (Good-Satisfactory)
Carlos Mendoza: 6.0 (Satisfactory)
```

### Column 7: Outstanding Accomplishments (0-5)

**What It Shows:**
- Special achievements and recognitions
- Awards or notable contributions
- Beyond routine job duties

**Scoring:**
```
5: Multiple major accomplishments/awards
4: One major accomplishment or 2+ minor
3: One accomplishment or significant contribution
2: Some recognition or achievement
1: Minor accomplishment or participation
0: No notable accomplishments
```

**Example:**
```
Maria Santos: 4.0 (Multiple awards)
Juan Dela Cruz: 3.0 (One accomplishment)
Ana Reyes: 2.0 (Some recognition)
Carlos Mendoza: 1.0 (Minor achievement)
```

### Column 8: Application of Education (0-10)

**What It Shows:**
- How applicant applies education in job
- Uses knowledge daily
- Leverages qualifications effectively

**Scoring:**
```
10: Excellent use of education, demonstrates mastery
8-9: Applies education regularly and effectively
6-7: Demonstrates ability to apply education
4-5: Some application of education
2-3: Minimal application of education
0-1: Fails to apply education or irrelevant education
```

**Example:**
```
Maria Santos: 8.0 (Excellent application)
Juan Dela Cruz: 7.0 (Regular application)
Ana Reyes: 6.0 (Demonstrates ability)
Carlos Mendoza: 5.0 (Some application)
```

### Column 9: Application of L&D (0-10)

**What It Shows:**
- How applicant applies learning and development
- Uses training in job
- Demonstrates continuous learning

**Scoring:**
```
10: Excellent application of learning
8-9: Regularly applies learning on job
6-7: Shows application of learning
4-5: Some application of learning
2-3: Minimal application of learning
0-1: No application of learning
```

**Example:**
```
Maria Santos: 7.0 (Regular application)
Juan Dela Cruz: 6.0 (Shows application)
Ana Reyes: 5.0 (Some application)
Carlos Mendoza: 4.0 (Minimal application)
```

### Column 10: Potential (0-10)

**What It Shows:**
- Growth and development potential
- Capability for higher positions
- Leadership possibility
- Future advancement prospect

**Scoring:**
```
10: Excellent potential for advancement
8-9: High potential for growth
6-7: Good potential for development
4-5: Moderate potential
2-3: Limited potential
0-1: No apparent potential
```

**Example:**
```
Maria Santos: 9.0 (Excellent potential)
Juan Dela Cruz: 8.0 (High potential)
Ana Reyes: 7.0 (Good potential)
Carlos Mendoza: 6.0 (Moderate potential)
```

### Column 11: Total

**What It Shows:**
- Weighted total of all criteria
- Final score used for ranking
- Range: 0.00 to 100.00

**Calculation:**
```
Total = (E×0.15) + (T×0.05) + (EX×0.20) + (P×0.25) + 
        (A×0.05) + (AE×0.10) + (AL×0.10) + (PO×0.10)

Where:
E = Education score
T = Training score
EX = Experience score
P = Performance score
A = Accomplishments score
AE = Application of Education
AL = Application of L&D
PO = Potential score
```

**Example:**
```
Maria Santos: 9.38
Juan Dela Cruz: 8.73
Ana Reyes: 8.08
Carlos Mendoza: 7.25
```

**Formatting:**
- Always 2 decimal places
- Bold or highlighted
- Usually right-aligned

### Column 12: Rank

**What It Shows:**
- Position ranking (1st, 2nd, 3rd, etc.)
- Highest score = Rank 1
- Lower score = Higher rank number

**Example:**
```
Maria Santos: 1  (Highest score)
Juan Dela Cruz: 2
Ana Reyes: 3
Carlos Mendoza: 4  (Lowest score)
```

**Important Notes:**
- Ranking is PER POSITION (not system-wide)
- Same applicant can have different ranks for different positions
- Ties get same rank (next rank skips)

### Column 13: Remarks

**What It Shows:**
- Additional notes or comments
- Clarifications or context
- Usually empty for standard CAR
- Used for special circumstances

**Examples:**
```
(empty)
"Specialized in digital learning"
"First in class academically"
"Bilingual capability"
"Leadership experience"
```

## Ranking System

### How Rankings Are Calculated

**Rank Formula:**

```
Within each position:
1. Sort all applicants by Total Score (highest first)
2. Assign Rank 1 to highest score
3. Assign Rank 2 to next highest
4. Continue for all applicants
```

### Ranking Example

**Position: Information and Communications Technology**

```
Applicant                Score    Rank
─────────────────────────────────────────
Maria Santos             9.38     1     ← Highest score
Juan Dela Cruz           8.73     2
Ana Reyes                8.08     3
Carlos Mendoza           7.25     4     ← Lowest score
```

### Handling Ties

**When Two Scores Are Identical:**

```
Example: Scores 8.50, 8.50, 7.75

Applicant 1              8.50     1     ← First position
Applicant 2              8.50     1     ← Same rank (tied)
Applicant 3              7.75     3     ← Next rank skips 2
```

**Both tied applicants are equally qualified**

### Multiple Positions

**Same Applicant in Different Positions:**

```
Position 1 (ICT Teacher):
├─ Maria Santos: Rank 1 (Score 9.38)
├─ Juan Dela Cruz: Rank 2 (Score 8.73)
└─ Ana Reyes: Rank 3 (Score 8.08)

Position 2 (Science Teacher):
├─ Ana Reyes: Rank 1 (Score 9.15)  ← Different position, different rank
├─ Maria Santos: Rank 2 (Score 8.90)
└─ Carlos Mendoza: Rank 3 (Score 7.25)
```

**Note:** Ana Reyes is Rank 3 for ICT but Rank 1 for Science

### Decision Making

**Typical Hiring Decisions:**

```
Rank 1: ✅ Definitely hire
        - Best qualified
        - Highest score
        - Clear first choice

Rank 2: ✅ Hire if budget allows
        - Very close to Rank 1
        - Backup option
        - Good alternative

Rank 3+: ⚠️ Consider if needed
        - Only if additional positions open
        - Significant gap from Rank 1
        - Reserve list
```

## Signature Section

```

Chairperson: _________________________
             (Signature over Printed Name)

Member 1:    _________________________
             (Signature over Printed Name)

Member 2:    _________________________
             (Signature over Printed Name)

```

### Why Signatures Matter

- ✅ Confirms document authenticity
- ✅ Shows approval by authorized personnel
- ✅ Official authorization for hiring decisions
- ✅ Legal record of who approved
- ✅ Audit trail for compliance

### Who Signs?

- **Chairperson:** Head of evaluation committee
- **Members:** Other evaluators who assessed applicants
- **HR Representative:** May also sign
- **Department Head:** May approve

## Print and Export

### Printing CAR

**Steps:**
1. View CAR details
2. Click "Print" button
3. Choose printer and settings
4. Select "Landscape" orientation
5. Adjust margins if needed (0.5")
6. Preview to verify all content fits
7. Click "Print"

**Print Tips:**
- ✅ Use landscape (not portrait)
- ✅ Use A4 paper
- ✅ Check margins (0.5" minimum)
- ✅ Verify all columns fit on one page
- ✅ Test print preview first

### Exporting to CSV

**Steps:**
1. View CAR details
2. Click "Export" or "Download CSV" button
3. Choose save location
4. File downloads (usually .csv extension)
5. Open in Excel or Google Sheets

**After Export:**
- All data in spreadsheet format
- Formulas work (can recalculate)
- Can create charts and graphs
- Can filter and sort
- Can share with others
- Can backup data

### File Formats

| Format | Best For | How To Create |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Print (PDF) | Official document, signatures, archiving | Use print to PDF |
| CSV | Analysis, Excel, backup | Use export button |
| PDF | Sharing, emailing, permanent record | Print to PDF |
| HTML | Viewing in browser, web sharing | Built-in export |
